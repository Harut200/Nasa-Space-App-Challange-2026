## 2. Say / do not say

**Say**
- "We checked every headline number against data our detector never used: Landsat, a NASA altimeter, river gauges, weather stations."
- "Independent data corrected our own story three times." (a) The flood did not arrive late. The lake rose sharply from early July and peaked 24-29 Sept. Our radar saw the July rise as water under vegetation, and our open water kept growing after the lake started to fall. (b) The two "windy" Sevan dates were not simply wind: 31 Aug followed a rain night (29 mm in 48 h). We now say "rough-surface dates". (c) Rain did not cause the odd HV channel on 6 Oct in Armenia (at most 1 mm). Source: `docs/RESULTS.md`, `docs/eda/validation-2026.md`.
- "The app shows maps made from free 180 m preview images. We re-ran Tonle Sap on calibrated 80 m data and it fits better." (`docs/eda/calibrated.md`)
- "Water size is known to about plus or minus 20 %."
- "Coherence shows the farming season in the Ararat Valley." / "Ground motion: nothing above our +/-15 mm limit over four months."
- "Radar sees through cloud and at night." (`docs/research/science-brief.md`, section 1)
- "AI was used for research, planning, code, analysis and first drafts, and we disclose it." (`README.md`)
- "NISAR's 12-day pair spanning the M7.0 Tibet earthquake of 22 October shows the ground moving by more than a metre (127 cm peak to peak along the radar line of sight, within 45 km). The same track a fortnight earlier is flat to under 1 cm in the far field and 5 cm peak to peak." (`docs/eda/quake.md`, "What may and may not be claimed")
- "For Venezuela and Panama, two look directions show the pattern flipping sign, as horizontal strike-slip motion would. These are provisional measurements; where we looked at earthquakes in the sea or too small to see, we report that nothing was detected."
- Venezuela only, with "approximately": "Combining the two views gives roughly 40 cm of east-west motion and under 10 cm vertical." (`quake.md`)

**Do not say**
- Our models beat NASA's, or any model we did not test. We tested a few public networks (see Q on deep learning) and none beat our fusion; that is all we can say.
- A bare "N continents". Name them: Asia, Africa, South America, North America (Panama, Hawaii), Europe (Etna); Armenia sits on the Europe-Asia boundary.
- That Inner Niger, Lake Chad or Pantanal are solved because of 97-99 % accuracy: that was measured on certain pixels only. Say "agree with" or "consistent with" NASA's OPERA product, never "better than NASA".
- That NISAR sees M5 earthquakes in general; that the Venezuela M7.2 and M7.5 are separate ruptures; whether a ground event is lava, ash or debris, or its volume.
- That subsidence was measured. It was not detected (limit +/-15 mm over 120 days).
- That per-field harvest dates were mapped. The change is a season-long low, not a one-day event.
- That soil moisture shows irrigation. It does not (`docs/eda/insar.md`, summary table).
- Preview-image areas are calibrated measurements. The app numbers come from uncalibrated 8-bit previews.
- That the flood "arrived late".
- That we measured damage, casualties or any effect on people from an earthquake, a fire, a storm or a volcano; or a burnt area, a deforestation area or a crop area (our fire numbers are "area of changed radar echo" or "lost coherence").
- That our maps beat NASA's products. Where we compared (OPERA), we agree in some places and are wrong in others.
- That Rondonia shows forest clearing (it does not clearly; the place is out of the app), or that the Gironde fire is clear by brightness (it fades; it is clear only by lost coherence).
- The fault plane, slip or rupture length of any earthquake; that one look direction tells vertical from horizontal motion (Tibet, Kumamoto, Panama); that the Venezuela east/up split is precise.
- That the earthquake maps are validated against GNSS, Sentinel-1 or any other data, or that the sign (toward or away from the satellite) is confirmed. The products are PROVISIONAL, partly urgent-response.
- That the Panama pattern is complete (the frames end at the epicentre), that the M7.5 and M7.2 Venezuela signals are separated, or that NISAR "failed" at Flores, Mexico, Sanriku or Qinghai (the ground was offshore or the signal too small: say so).
- Extreme single pixels as results (percentiles are), or the earthquake maps as a time series (one pair per event).
- That the calibrated result works everywhere. It is one place (Tonle Sap) so far, and it is in the app as `tonle-sap-cal`.
- That 98.9 % of our water is "correct". It is inside the 1984-2021 Landsat maximum extent, which is a plausibility check (the current app map: 97.0 %, `docs/eda/fusion2.md`).

## 3. Likely judge questions

1. **Is this calibrated data?** The app shows maps built from NISAR's free 180 m preview images, which are display brightness, not backscatter, and the app says so. We also re-ran Tonle Sap on calibrated 80 m data (provisional calibration, ten dates): 5,625 km² on 17 Oct, within 5.9 % of the independent estimate on average. It is in the app as its own place (about 5,660 km² on its own window; about 5,600 either way) and the same thresholds do not work at Lake Sevan (different radar mode, higher noise floor). (`docs/eda/calibrated.md`, section 9)
2. **How do you know the flood size?** We do not know it exactly. We have an independent estimate from the altimeter lake level and 38 years of Landsat (5,421 km² on 17 Oct, range 4,198-7,222); our 5,046 km² (range 4,196-6,051) falls inside it. The detector's floodplain threshold was tuned to that same estimate, so the 8.5 % error is partly in-sample. (`docs/RESULTS.md`, Limits)
3. **Why not deep learning?** We tried and measured it. A large pretrained network scored IoU 0.21 against 0.65 for a Gaussian mixture; a flood network from Microsoft matched its own threshold rule; the ALOS-2 L-band model scored 0.91 at Sevan against 0.99; learned speckle filters changed nothing beyond noise. A network trained on Landsat labels matched the maths on unseen places but did not beat the fusion we use. (`docs/RESULTS.md`, "What we tried that did not help"; `docs/eda/supervised.md`)
4. **What is new here?** A season of NISAR radar made explorable by anyone, with every number checked against independent data and with the corrections shown. Also a detector that sets its own thresholds per scene, and dated flood fronts through the outage. We do not claim a new radar method beyond this. (`docs/RESULTS.md`)
5. **What would you do with a year of NISAR?** Run the calibrated pipeline for all places, and repeat the ground-motion test: our four-month limit is +/-15 mm, which would be about 8 mm per year with a full year. (`docs/eda/insar.md`, section 7)
6. **How was AI used?** The team did the research, the data analysis and the planning, and designed the detection approach: which radar measurements to use, the formulas, the control tests and the experiments, and what counts as a result. Harutyun Kesablyan and Abel Babayan led detection, data analysis and science; Arameh Haghverdian led the design of the app's back end and front end. AI tools (Claude) were used as an assistant under the team's direction: to help with research, write code, run analyses and draft text. The team reviewed the code, the results and the text. The repository labels this. (`README.md`, "Use of AI")
7. **How do you know it is not just wind or rain?** At Sevan we checked weather data: 31 Aug followed a rain night, 12 Sep had a steady 2-4 m/s breeze, and neither shows strong wind. So we call them "rough-surface dates" and our final detector keeps the lake at about 1,240 km² on both. (`docs/eda/validation-2026.md`)
8. **What about the outage?** No passes exist from 27 Jul to 10 Aug 2026. The app shows the gap and does not invent a pass; a hidden-pass test says our front dating beats "nothing changed" (82 against 161 km²). (`docs/eda/novel.md`)
9. **Can radar see ground motion or irrigation?** Not in our data. Ground motion at Ararat: nothing above +/-15 mm. Soil moisture: the product did not separate irrigated land from dry hills. Coherence did show the farming season. (`docs/eda/insar.md`)
10. **Who is this for?** Teachers, reporters and anyone curious about a region: a dated, plain-language view of one radar season. (deck notes, slides 6-7; no user study was done.)
11. **Did you validate the earthquake maps?** Not against an independent measurement: we did no GNSS, Sentinel-1 or other-satellite comparison. The evidence is that a pair from before each earthquake on the same track is flat (5 to 21 cm against 38 to 133 cm for the large events) and that the pattern sits on the USGS epicentre and fits its mechanism in type and order of magnitude. The sign convention is unconfirmed and the products are provisional. (`docs/eda/quake.md`, Limits)
12. **Why were eight earthquakes not seen?** Each for its own reason, listed in `docs/eda/quake.md`: Flores M7.8 and Mexico M7.3 had epicentres in the sea (Mexico: under 1 % valid pixels within 60 km), Sanriku M6.9 was offshore and 34 to 45 km deep (1.4 % valid pixels), Qinghai M5.8 and four more events of M5.3 to 5.7 are too small to expect more than a few centimetres (of seven small events, one clear, one marginal, five nothing). That is a statement about those scenes, not a failure of NISAR; we do not claim it could not be done with other data.

13. **Does it agree with NASA's own water product?** Where the scene is mostly open water, yes: at Lake Sevan and Tonle Sap our maps agree with NASA's OPERA DSWx-S1 product on 97.5 % of pixels (IoU 0.84). Over 101 place-dates at 15 places the mean IoU is only 0.38, and in three places (Inner Niger, Lake Chad, Urmia) the comparison shows our map is wrong; this is agreement between two products, not accuracy, and we do not claim to be better than NASA (`docs/eda/opera.md`).
14. **Where does it fail?** Three places where the OPERA comparison shows our map is wrong: Inner Niger (we map about four times OPERA's open water; 98 % of our extra water is dry in OPERA), Lake Chad (we find about half) and Lake Urmia (wet salt flat called water). Deltas, the Pantanal and the Sudd are weak because narrow or vegetated water is missed. The earlier 97-99 % accuracies at some of these places were measured only on certain pixels. (`docs/eda/opera.md`)

## 4. Limits, in six lines

1. The app uses uncalibrated 180 m previews; calibrated results exist for Tonle Sap only (in the app as `tonle-sap-cal`) and are provisional.
2. Flood size is known to about plus or minus 20 %, its outline less well (only 76 % of our water lies inside the independent flood map); no 2026 ground truth exists for the floodplain.
3. Water under vegetation (about 1,600-2,500 km² at the 30 Aug peak) is not validated.
4. One season, ten passes, a 24-day outage; no slow ground motion detected at Ararat; Ararat coherence is not in the app.
5. Earthquakes: one pair per event; line-of-sight only; no independent check; sign unconfirmed; PROVISIONAL products; eight of fifteen not detected, one marginal. Against NASA's OPERA product three places are wrong (Inner Niger, Lake Chad, Urmia) and mean IoU over 101 place-dates is 0.38.
6. Evidence layers and event counts: areas are of changed radar echo on uncalibrated previews; the control is not clean at four places; the event hunt is still running (counts as of 03:57); the visibility audit was not repeated after its fixes.
(`docs/RESULTS.md`, Limits; `docs/eda/calibrated.md`; `docs/eda/insar.md`; `docs/eda/quake.md`; `docs/eda/burn.md`)

## 5. Space Apps judging criteria (scored 1-5 each)

Source: `docs/research/submission-rules.md` (paraphrase of the official pages; reread the originals).

- **Impact:** The Tonle Sap flood, which connects the lake with forests where fish feed (app story text, `src/app/public/data/tonle-sap/manifest.json`), becomes something a teacher or reporter can show in 30 seconds. (No user numbers; do not claim reach.)
- **Creativity:** The dance metaphor made literal: each pixel's date of change as one colour image, flood order as a rough height map, the front dated through the outage.
- **Validity:** Every headline is checked against independent data (Landsat, altimeter, gauges, weather), corrections are published, limits are stated, and the false-alarm rate is measured.
- **Relevance:** It answers "Dancing with the SARs" directly: NISAR L-band passes, June-October 2026, surface change shown in an app.
- **Presentation:** A 30 s video and a 7-slide deck with the same story: play the flood, show Sevan standing still, state the limits.

Other required items to check before submitting (same file): demo link public with no login; NASA data listed; licence file (MIT, in the repo); AI use disclosed; team names and challenge on screen; "Submit for Judging" pressed before 23:59 on 15 Nov.
