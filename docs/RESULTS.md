## What the project shows

From ten free preview images of the new NASA-ISRO NISAR L-band radar (June to October 2026), we map open water, water under vegetation and lasting change for 18 water and farmland places, show three wildfires as a change of radar brightness, and follow the 2026 Tonle Sap flood pass by pass. From NISAR interferometric pairs we also map the ground movement of six earthquakes and ten other ground events seen as loss of coherence (two volcanoes, a rock-ice collapse, six wildfires and the Atacama snow and rain storms). The app has 37 places in 9 classes (`summary.json`, `categories`): Floods 8, Lakes 4, Wetlands and deltas 4, Farmland 2, Wildfires 9, Earthquakes 6, Volcanoes 2, Landslides 1, Storms 1. Of the nine wildfires, three are seen by brightness change and six by lost coherence (three fires are seen both ways). Rondônia was taken out of the app (`areas/rondonia/area.json`, `in_app: false`): no layer shows new clearing. The places lie in Asia, Africa, South America (Brazil, Venezuela, Chile), North America (Canada, Panama, Hawaii) and Europe (Spain, France, Etna); Armenia sits on the Europe–Asia boundary. Most headline numbers below were checked against data our detector never used: 38 years of Landsat water maps, a NASA satellite altimeter, river gauges, weather stations and an elevation model. The comparison with NASA's own OPERA water product is the exception to read first (next table and Limits): it shows places where our maps are wrong.

## What independent data corrected

* **The Tonle Sap flood did not "arrive late".** We had said that open water barely grew until late July and that the biggest rise came between 11 and 23 September. The gauge and the altimeter show the lake rising sharply from early July and peaking on 24–29 September, on time or early. Our open-water layer kept growing until 17 October while the lake was already falling. The early rise is in our data, but as water under vegetation, not as open water.
* **The two "windy" Sevan dates were not simply wind.** 31 August followed a rain night (29 mm in 48 hours at the shore station); 12 September was dry with a steady 2–4 m/s breeze. No source shows wind above about 3 m/s at pass time on either date. We now call these "rough-surface dates".
* **We withdrew our own Sheveluch result.** A ring of lost coherence round the Sheveluch domes in the 22 Sep–4 Oct pair was exported as a volcanic event; an elevation test (SRTM heights; loss 8 / 32 / 60 / 77 % at 600–900 / 900–1200 / 1200–1500 / 1500–1800 m, the same in the neighbouring Klyuchevskoy group, no recovery) showed first snow. It is now marginal and not in the app (`areas/sheveluch-2026/area.json`, `in_app: false`). Langtang passed the same test ([events.md](eda/events.md)).
* **Rain did not cause the odd HV channel on 6 October in Armenia** (at most 1 mm in the 48 hours before the pass). An instrument or processing effect is the more likely cause; our automatic quality control now flags that channel and leaves it out of change detection.

## Limits

* **Our water maps disagree with NASA's OPERA product in specific places** ([opera.md](eda/opera.md)): Inner Niger (we map about four times OPERA's open water; 98 % of our extra water is dry in OPERA), Lake Chad (we find about half), Lake Urmia (false water on wet salt flat); the Mekong and Nile deltas, the Pantanal and the Sudd are weak, and at Okavango and Sindh-Indus our maps hold no open water. Over 101 place-dates the mean IoU is 0.38. The 97–99 % balanced accuracies quoted for these places were measured only on pixels where the answer is certain (always or never water), so they do not show these places are solved. This is agreement with another product, not accuracy; OPERA is itself a model and misses water under forest.
* **The app shows mostly uncalibrated previews**: 8-bit display brightness at about 180 m per pixel, not calibrated backscatter. Calibrated 80 m data for Tonle Sap (ten dates) are analysed ([calibrated.md](eda/calibrated.md)) and shown as the place `tonle-sap-cal`; no other calibrated data are in the app. The same dB water thresholds give 0 km² at Lake Sevan (the Armenian product has a noise floor 7–8 dB higher), the calibration is PROVISIONAL, and the omnibus change test over-flags on real data without a minimum effect size.
* **The size of the Tonle Sap flood is known to about ±20 %, its exact outline less well.** The app shows 5,046 km² of open water on 17 October (range 4,196–6,051). The detector's floodplain threshold was fitted to the same independent estimate it is scored against, so the 8.5 % area error is partly in-sample; and only 76 % of that water lies inside the independent flood map (the stricter math detector: 3,837 km², 86 % inside). The total is better than the location. Details: [fusion2.md](eda/fusion2.md), [hydro.md](eda/hydro.md), [tuning.md](eda/tuning.md).
* **No 2026 ground truth for the floodplain.** Landsat shows where water has been (1984–2021), not where it was on a given day of 2026; the altimeter checks the timing, not the extent.
* **Water under vegetation**: about 1,600–2,500 km² at the 30 August peak, depending on one context setting; not validated, because no reference map exists.
* **Ground events** ([events.md](eda/events.md)): loss of coherence says only that the ground can no longer be compared between the two passes; it gives no volume, no lava/ash/debris/snow/burnt-forest distinction, no burnt area or damage, no date inside the pair and no motion. We write "lost coherence", not "burnt". New do-not-claim items (`events.md`): a 24- or 36-day event pair compared with a 12-day control compares only approximately; an autumn loss of coherence in mountains needs an elevation test before it is called volcanic; the cause of the Atacama loss (rain, snow, mud) is not separable; Hautes Fagnes is a clear signal not exported because the fire class is capped, and Rowe Creek's patch is cut by the window edge. Sheveluch shows why an autumn mountain result needs an elevation test.
* **Before/after evidence layers** ([burn.md](eda/burn.md)): areas are "area of changed radar echo" on uncalibrated previews; the control is not clean at Thunder Bay 36, Sindh-Indus, Poyang and Urmia; the Thunder Bay area is 0 at twice the threshold and 1,875 km² against 3,139 published; Okavango uses one later pass. A darker or brighter echo is consistent with the named cause but could be harvest, snow, soil moisture, rain or a radiometric difference between passes.
* **Visibility audit** ([visibility-audit.md](eda/visibility-audit.md)): measured before the fixes and not repeated; 31 of the 37 places (not the seven added later); Avila's water layers are blank.
* **Disaster events from previews** ([events-2026.md](research/events-2026.md)): the detector sees events only where mapped water jumps by hundreds of km² over weeks; small and narrow floods vanish at 180 m. Counts are from the 00:50 summary of that file.
* **Earthquakes** ([quake.md](eda/quake.md)): the Venezuela M7.2 and M7.5 events cannot be separated; "NISAR sees M5 earthquakes" is not supported (of seven small events, 1 clear, 1 marginal, 5 nothing); one pair per event, not a time series; line-of-sight movement only (one look direction mixes up, east and north); no independent check (GNSS, Sentinel-1) and an unconfirmed sign convention; PROVISIONAL, partly urgent-response products; near-fault pixels are the most decorrelated and extreme pixels are not results; Panama's frames end at the epicentre; the Kumamoto control pair (12 days) is shorter than its event pair (24 days); only Venezuela has an east/up split, and it is approximate. We do not claim damage, casualties, fault plane, slip or rupture length.
* **One season** of one year, ten passes, with a 24-day outage (27 July – 10 August).
